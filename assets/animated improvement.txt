Yes, bro. **A lot more than you probably think.** But there is one important distinction.

We can make the **entire animated environment procedural**, meaning the browser itself creates the scenery using HTML/CSS/SVG/Canvas/JavaScript. No stock videos, no downloaded city backgrounds, no external animation packs, no Lottie files, no Three.js models required.

So things like:

* 🌙 moon, stars, clouds and changing sky
* 🌅 dawn transitioning into morning
* ☀️ sunlight and atmospheric haze
* 🏙️ buildings, roads, poles, wires, traffic silhouettes
* 🚇 a moving metro and passing scenery
* 🚌 bus and 🛺 auto
* 🌳 trees, fields, ponds and village scenery
* 🌫️ fog, dust, light rays and particles
* 🏪 shops, market streets and moving people silhouettes
* 🛏️ room, fan shadow, curtains and lighting
* ✉️ desk, paper, pen and thread environment
* ✨ finale particles, light bloom and environmental effects

can all be constructed by code.

And actually, **for this project I think procedural animation is preferable** because it lets us control the cinematography continuously. The sky can literally transition from 4:30 AM to 7:30 AM instead of cutting between separate backgrounds.

The limitation is visual complexity.

We can make a beautiful **stylized animated short film** this way. We cannot realistically expect pure CSS/SVG to produce a photorealistic Bollywood-quality metro carriage with detailed humans and physically accurate reflections. 😭

But I don't think we need that.

Our target should be something like:

**2D/2.5D illustrated cinematic world + depth + parallax + camera movement + lighting + particles + carefully choreographed motion.**

That can look *extremely* good.

And there's another advantage: **performance.**

Since Anwesha's phone is a Realme 11 5G, I would actually prefer intelligently generated/vector environments over throwing 100 MB of videos at it. We can keep the scenes lightweight while still making them feel alive.

The architecture I'd want is roughly:

**Scene environment**
→ generated SVG/CSS/Canvas elements

**Camera**
→ GSAP transforms, scale, position, parallax

**Atmosphere**
→ gradients, blur, particles, fog, lighting

**Characters/vehicles**
→ simple vector silhouettes or layered SVGs

**Cinematography**
→ scripted timelines controlling all of the above

**Your actual photos**
→ real image assets

**Your actual audio**
→ local audio assets

So we're not saying **"zero assets whatsoever."**

The things that are inherently personal or photographic should remain real:

**your photos + your audio + actual Rakhi/gift imagery.**

Everything around them can be generated by the browser.

> **Real things belong to the story. Generated things belong to the world.**

Your photographs are real.
Your gifts are real.
Your letter is real.
Your music is yours.
