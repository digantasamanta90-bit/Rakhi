And I agree with your assessment almost completely.

The biggest problem is not that Scenes 12–14 are “bad.” They are actually carrying the right content. **They are just using the wrong medium for that content.** Scenes 1–9 use animation to communicate. Scenes 10–14 start using UI components to communicate. That is why they suddenly feel like PPT slides.

### First: Scene 9 → 10

You said the transition itself is good, but a photograph overlaps the text underneath.

That tells me the transition concept is fine. **The composition is wrong.**

Scene 10 is currently trying to fit:

* three fairly large photographs
* a connecting thread
* two text blocks
* captions
* depth
* interaction

into essentially one viewport.

The center/bottom portrait is especially problematic because it occupies the same vertical territory as the narrative text.

So I wouldn't “fix the overlap” merely by moving the text 20px upward.

I'd change the composition logic.

The photograph should have its **own moment**.

For example:

> photograph appears → camera moves toward it → memory text appears → photograph drifts away → next photograph takes focus

rather than:

> three photos remain visible while text sits underneath them.

That gives us room and feels much more cinematic.

# And your point about text is absolutely correct

The solution isn't:

**“use less text.”**

The solution is:

**“stop displaying text like webpage content.”**

Scenes 1–9 already taught us the correct technique.

Text should arrive as:

* a line
* a thought
* a timestamp
* handwriting
* a message
* a reveal
* a whispered sentence

Then disappear or transform.

So instead of:

> paragraph paragraph paragraph paragraph

we do:

**“I had imagined this differently.”**

pause

**“You were waiting.”**

pause

**“And I wasn't there.”**

pause

Then the animation takes over.

This is exactly what you meant by the first scenes having revealing animation.

# There's another issue: too many things are visible simultaneously

That's the silent killer.

Cinematic scenes generally have:

**one focal object + supporting atmosphere.**

The later scenes have:

**five focal objects + supporting text + buttons + metadata + borders + cards + labels.**

The eye doesn't know where to look.

So for every scene from now on I'd enforce:

> **At any given moment, one thing is the star.**

Scene 10 → photograph.

Scene 11 → gift.

Research → investigation.

Scene 12 → room interaction.

Scene 13 → letter.

Scene 14 → thread.

Scene 15 → apology.

Scene 16 → Rakhi.

That's it.

# One more thing I'd change

I wouldn't automatically make every additional beat another “scene.”

Some should be **continuous sub-sequences**.

For example:

`Scene 11`

Gifts appear

→ KitKat becomes focus

→ research button emerges

→ research opens as a cinematic overlay

→ research ends

→ KitKat returns to its place

→ Bellavita takes focus

→ Scene 12

That feels much more fluid than adding a navigation step every 30 seconds.

So the architecture should think in:

**Scenes → Sequences → Shots**

rather than just:

**Scene 1 → Scene 2 → Scene 3**

That's how we keep the experience from becoming a fancy slideshow.
